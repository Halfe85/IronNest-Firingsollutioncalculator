How to calculate elevation and powder charges in IRON NEST

This guide explains the community-reported firing equation used by Iron Nest FCC, how to turn bearing and range into a firing card, and what to do if your shell misses. These calculations are only for the game, not real artillery.

Find the information you need

Before starting, note the Iron Nest grid, the target bearing in degrees, and the distance in kilometres. You can obtain the bearing and distance directly from mission intelligence, or locate the target first in the Tactical Plotter guide when the reports name multiple spotters.

Open Iron Nest FCC and choose Calculate. Confirm the Iron Nest grid using the letter, row, and two subcell selectors. Enter bearing and distance, select the shell and left or right cannon, then check the displayed result.

The elevation formula

The implemented, community-reported game rule is:

Elevation (degrees) = distance (km) × 12 ÷ powder charges

For example, for a target 12 km away using 3 charges, elevation is 48°. For 10 km with two charges, elevation reaches the game's 60° maximum.

The calculator offers 1–6 powder charges, with a maximum modeled reach of 5 km per charge. Selecting Auto uses the lowest charge that can reach the entered range. Select a charge manually if the mission or your firing plan calls for a different valid angle.

Bearing, grid and shell type

Bearing is a compass direction, not elevation. In the app, 0° points north, 90° east, 180° south and 270° west. If your Iron Nest location has been confirmed, the calculator can also project the entered bearing and range to an approximate target grid.

Choose the correct shell type from the list and assign a Left or Right cannon. The shell label is retained with the solution; shell selection does not change the shared elevation equation. Read mission instructions to decide which ammunition is appropriate.

Save and use a firing solution

Press + FIRE SOLUTION to save a card in the Fire Solutions workspace. It shows bearing, elevation, charges, shell, cannon and the projected grid. The compact card expands on hover on a desktop or on tap on a phone.

Press HIT to archive a confirmed target under Neutralized. If you miss, press MISS to open the correction tool. A report can set a new target grid, provide a new bearing and distance from Iron Nest, record the observed shell-impact grid, or describe the target by bearing and distance from the impact point. These are different types of correction and should not be confused.

What the result does not guarantee

Game coordinates are rounded to map subdivisions of roughly 100 m, and some intelligence reports are only approximate. The displayed grid may not be the exact impact point. A calculated elevation does not by itself determine shell flight time; the app treats that separately when planning moving-target shots.

For multi-spotter observations and uncertain targets, use the spotter triangulation guide. For a timed train or moving ship, see the waypoint interception guide.

Unofficial tool. IRON NEST is a video game. This calculator and its community-derived equations are for gameplay only. Rounding, map orientation and flight-time estimates can differ from what you observe in-game.
