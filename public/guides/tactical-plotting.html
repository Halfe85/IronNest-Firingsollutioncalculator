How to triangulate targets from Iron Nest spotter reports

Some IRON NEST objectives describe an unknown location relative to known spotters or earlier reference points. Iron Nest FCC plots those observations geometrically so you can turn the intelligence into a grid and eventually a firing solution. The process works without mission-specific templates.

Start with fixed locations

Open the Iron Nest calculator, select Tactical Plotter and switch to Observation. Enter the grid position of your Iron Nest, then add each reported spotter with a known coordinate. Name spotters clearly—especially when a mission distinguishes Spotter #1 from Spotter #2.

The assumed in-game grid has columns A–T from left to right and rows 1–10 from bottom to top. A coordinate such as C2 5:6 consists of a letter, row, and two 0–9 subdivisions. Each full cell is modeled as 1 km and each subdivision as 100 m. Verify the grid orientation on your own mission map.

Add reports to references and targets

Create a Reference or Target point. Inside its modal, add a report and choose the explicit FROM source. It might be a spotter, another reference, or an already located target. The TYPE can be:

  • Bearing — an angle measured clockwise from north, such as 077°.
  • Range — the distance in kilometres, such as 5.73 km.
  • Compass — a direction such as NE, SSW or WNW; this is less precise than a numerical bearing.

Save each source accurately. “077° from Spotter 1” and “077° from Spotter 2” define entirely different lines.

Why two observations may produce several answers

Two forward bearing lines often meet at one point, if they intersect at all. A bearing ray and distance circle can intersect twice, once, or not at all. Two distance circles can likewise produce zero, one or two candidates.

When the tool finds multiple valid points, inspect the plotted candidates and use any additional report to determine which candidate matches your intelligence. Do not assume the first candidate is correct. Subsequent dependent reference points remain uncertain until you select a location.

Handling impossible or approximate intersections

Game reports and plotted coordinates can contain small rounding differences. When a bearing narrowly misses a reported distance circle within the solver's defined tolerance, the tool can produce an estimated location marked ≈. That approximation also affects targets derived from the reference.

But some contradictions are not ordinary rounding: two bearing rays may only intersect behind the observers, run nearly parallel, or point to a location outside the playable map. The solver reports a conflict instead of inventing a target. Check that bearings belong to the correct spotters, that coordinates were entered correctly, and that the reported numbers were transcribed accurately. It does not secretly swap bearings or reverse them.

From plotted target to firing solution

Once a target is located, open its point modal, select the shell and cannon, then use Fire Solution. The app calculates the distance and bearing from Iron Nest to that target, followed by the required charge and elevation. A located point is an estimate based on observations, so confirm the position against the game if there is uncertainty.

For a moving target that changes position with the in-game clock, use Waypoint mode instead of treating its last sighting as stationary. For the underlying firing-angle equation, see the firing solution guide.
